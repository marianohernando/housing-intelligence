How to read the timing line (D-252b): `ms` is the whole sheet, `upstream_ms` the time spent waiting for the sources, and `parts`
names each part that failed as stage:error class:ms. A part that worked is not listed, so a complete sheet says `parts=-`
and gives no time per step.

What it says:
  - The three degraded sheets failed only in the plan's tables 4 and 5 (`plan_uses`), by time (TimeoutError), not by a quick error,
    so D-252b's one retry never applied.
  - With D-252b the tables had the time left before the sheet's 15 s deadline: 6.6, 4.6 and 7.0 s, not a fixed 4 s, and still did
    not answer. The fixed 4 s of D-252 was not the cause, or not the only one.
  - Before the tables, the Catastro and layer 3 took about 8.2, 10.0 and 7.6 s (ms minus the tables' ms).
  - A degraded sheet now takes the whole budget (14.6–14.7 s), against 8.5–11.7 s with f806850; Almería 1 came complete in 13.4 s.

The cause (datamad-10, measured from Madrid after this run): the Ayuntamiento's ArcGIS server itself. A query of tables 4 and 5 with
a CODIGO it does not hold in its cache takes 6.5–10 s on the server (time to first byte 8.4, 10.4 and 7.0 s), and about 0.2 s once
the query is in its cache. That fits deploy-41's local diagnosis, where the tables answered in under 0.3 s: those queries had just
been made from Dublin. Line F is preparing D-252c: the two tables (40,021 + 1,328 rows) in a versioned snapshot, so the sheet no
longer asks for them live.

At the end, Almería 1 is in the CDN; Méndez Álvaro, Luis Misson and Mayor 61 are not (`no-store`).
